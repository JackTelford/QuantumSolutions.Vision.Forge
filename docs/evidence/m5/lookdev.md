What Blender adds over the three lane on this sheet, read off the thumbnails: a true soft shadow from the key on every look (long, to camera-left, with a soft edge; the three lane casts one shadow from a directional stand-in at 0.6 of the key and it barely shows on the dark looks); real reflections of the HDRI's softbox and the lights in the bottle's coat and in the cap, which burns to white on studio-clean; and the cyc's falloff by light transport, so the wall grades to gray at the top while the floor stays bright under the bottle. What it lacks: the halation pass (neon-tech blooms on the three still and not on the Blender one) and the motes, which are the three lane's seeded points. On exposure the Blender still is darker than the three still on every look, by 0.9 (warm-editorial) to 9.0 (obsidian) code values: the gray card agrees within 3 code values (`calibration.md`), so the difference is the shadows and the lower half of the bottle falling off under the key, not the light scalar; it is largest on the dark looks whose fill is weakest (obsidian 12:1, noir-title 16:1). On colour every look but one shifts all three channels down together with the luma; warm-editorial is the one look whose balance differs, the Blender still warmer (red 162 against 157, blue 124 against 134); the window rig's 3200 K fill is warm in both lanes, and which lane renders its warmth right is not measured here. The two stills outside their look's band on the Blender column are the two outside it on the three column (studio-clean, sunset-gradient), the question for the owner that `docs/evidence/m4/lookdev.md` already lists.
