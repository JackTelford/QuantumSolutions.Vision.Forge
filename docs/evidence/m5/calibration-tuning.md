# M5 calibration tuning log

How the blender lane's key power in `src/shared/blender/calibration-scalars.ts` (`blenderKeyPowerW`) was set on 2026-10-29 (Windows 11, Blender 5.2.3 LTS at `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe`, RTX 2060 driver 610.88, ffmpeg 9.0.2). The target is the 18 percent gray card at 117.3 ± 5 YAVG (ADR 0008 decision 2) under both view transforms with both GPU engines, with the studio_small_09 HDRI installed (1k, at `blenderKeyedHdriStrength` 0.05, turned toward the key) and with the flat world a machine without the HDRI gets. Every trial rendered the calibration scene of `src/shared/calibration/scene.ts` through `src/render/blender-shot.ts` (the resolver, the validated scene document, Blender, the predicate) under the product-studio rig at 0 EV with the lens focused on the card, as a still (denoised), and measured the card with the still gate's crop + signalstats filter (`src/calibration/measure.ts`, BT.709). The trials ran at 480x270 (Cycles OptiX 128 samples, EEVEE OpenGL 128 samples) through a scratch script that scaled every light's `powerW` in the resolved document by a factor before writing it; the adopted value was then rendered at 1920x1080 by `node scripts/probes/run.ts m5-calibration` (`calibration.md`).

A light's power is relative intensity × `blenderKeyPowerW` × (d / 1.2)²; the fixture's key stands at 1.2 m, so the factor below is the key's power over 150 W, the value module A handed over.

What the trials say about the lane:

- The handed-over 150 W was a guess from Blender's own defaults (a 1000 W point light lights a room); a 1.2 m square area light (1.44 m²) standing 1.2 m from the card needs about 18 W to put an 18 percent reflector at the display's middle gray. The interpolation from trials 1 and 2 assumed the encoded value follows the sRGB curve (the ratio 1.073 of the encoded values, raised to 2.4, gives 1.185 on the power), and the measured 118.2 at 18 W landed within a code value of the prediction.
- Unlike the three lane, which needed 2.57 (agx) and 3.25 (standard) because its `LinearToneMapping` and AgX put scene-linear 0.18 at different display values, Blender's AgX and Standard view transforms read the gray card within 0.1 of each other at every trial, so one value serves both tone mappings. They differ on the white patch (177 under AgX against 213 under Standard, the AgX roll-off ADR 0008 expects) and on the swatches.
- Cycles reads the card 2.7 code values above EEVEE at the same power (118.2 against 115.5). The difference is in the engines' light transport (EEVEE's area-light shading and soft shadows are approximations); 18 W splits the two around the target rather than centring either, so a still that passes the ladder's first rung (EEVEE) and a hero still from Cycles sit within 3 code values of each other on a gray card.
- The three lane under the same rig and HDRI reads 116.6 (agx) and 117.2 (standard) on the card, 173 / 211 on the white and 57 / 61 on the black (`docs/evidence/m4/calibration.md`); the Blender numbers (118.3 / 118.3, 177 / 213, 56 / 62) agree with it to within 4 code values on every neutral patch, so the irradiance rule and the white balance carry across the lanes.
- `blenderKeyedHdriStrength` stays at 0.05, the value taken from the three lane: the black patch reads 56 (Cycles agx) against the three lane's 57 under the same HDRI, and the flat world reads 55, so the HDRI's lift on the shadows matches the three lane's environment map without a change.
- The swatches turn under AgX much as they do in the three lane (blue −19.0° to −19.8°, green −15.4° to −16.6°, red +6.9° to +7.5°, yellow −3.4°, magenta −1.2°, cyan +0.7°; the three lane measured blue −18.3°, green −14.4°, red +4.2°) and keep their hue to 0.0° under Standard, where the Standard view is a plain sRGB encode; the tables are in `calibration.md`.
